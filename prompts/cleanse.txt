Edit the draft below for its intended reader and purpose. Infer the format and audience from the supplied context. Preserve the author's position and level of formality. A README needs a clear explanation and usable instructions; a sales page or script requires a different opening.

## Pass 1: Purpose and Flow

- Make the opening answer the reader's first question about the subject.
- Remove duplicate hooks and curiosity gaps that delay necessary information.
- Check that each sentence adds a useful point and follows logically from the previous one.
- Remove repeated metaphors, empty contrasts, and comparisons that do not explain anything; describe the relevant behavior directly.
- Explain jargon where the reader needs it, and keep conditions attached to claims.
- Keep a closing summary only if it serves the document's reader.

## Pass 2: Wording and Sentence Patterns

- Replace vague promotional wording with direct language. Review terms such as `delve`, `seamless`, `unlock`, `game-changing`, and `leverage` in context.
- Remove constructions such as `not just X, but Y`, `no X, no Y, just Z`, and adjacent sentences that merely relabel the same thing for different audiences. Preserve a contrast when the distinction is necessary to the explanation.
- Review repeated sentence shapes across paragraphs, not just individual words.
- Replace stacked hedges with a verb that reflects the author's actual certainty.
- Review self-answering questions and decorative lists. Keep useful questions, lists, punctuation, and technical terms when they communicate the point well.

## Pass 3: Context and Accuracy

- Read the result as a complete document to verify it suits the reader and format.
- Let sentence length follow the meaning. Do not force short sentences, fragments, contractions, opinions, deliberate mistakes, or other signs of personality.
- Use concrete details already in the source where they help. Do not add a number to every claim or turn every document into sales copy.
- Check that the edit has not overstated capabilities, removed a qualification, or changed the author's argument.

## Absolute Rules

- Never invent a fact, statistic, quote, customer, logo, rating, or result.
- If necessary information is missing, use a clear placeholder such as `[needs number]` and explain the gap in the notes rather than guessing.
- Preserve factual meaning and technical behavior. Flag a questionable claim in the notes instead of silently replacing it with an unsupported claim.
- Preserve HTML tags, Markdown structure, links, and placeholders exactly.
- Leave code blocks, commands, inline code, and intentionally quoted examples unchanged, as they may document the patterns being discussed.
- Keep the author's register, and do not add an opinion the author did not express.

## Output Format

Return the rewritten copy in full, with nothing before it. Follow it with a line containing:

```text
<<<SLOPBEAST-NOTES>>>
```

Include nothing else on that line. Below the marker, provide at most five bullets describing the substantive edits or unresolved concerns, with no preamble or restatement of these instructions.

The marker is parsed by a script. Emit it exactly once, alone on its line, with no bullets, quotes, or backticks around it—even if nothing was changed. Everything above the marker is returned as copy; everything below it is shown to the author as notes.
